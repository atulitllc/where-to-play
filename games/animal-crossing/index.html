Animal Crossing

  • 2001
  • GameCube and Nintendo 64
  • Nintendo
  • 6th generation

History

A town on a real-time clock, neighbors who move away, and letters instead of a win screen.

It is the first Animal Crossing released in the West, on GameCube. New Horizons and the handheld towns are separate games.

Animal Crossing is recorded here as a 2001 game. The lead system is GameCube, 6th generation, disc home console. GameCube is Nintendo's disc home console of the sixth generation. This page records the disc release, not a later reissue. Every other GameCube game in this catalog is on the GameCube page.

This is one page for the same game on more than one Nintendo system. Also named here: Nintendo 64. A remake, or a release that is a different work, gets its own page. The year goes in the name when two slugs would otherwise collide.

The credit chip on this page reads Nintendo. RAWG lists no developer name and no publisher name. If those names disagree with the chip, both stay on the page so the record can be checked.

RAWG's genre labels for this record are RPG, Simulation. They are a filing aid, not a review written for this page, and not a description of how the game feels in your hands.

The date field is 2001-04-14; the user rating is 4.17 out of 5 from 91 ratings; the ESRB label on the card is Everyone. Those fields belong to RAWG. This page does not add a score of its own.

Series context: this title belongs with Animal Crossing. Other Animal Crossing pages in the catalog run from 2001 to 2020, including Animal Crossing: Wild World, Animal Crossing: City Folk, Animal Crossing: New Leaf, Animal Crossing: New Horizons.

In this catalog, Animal Crossing is the family name. Same franchise lists spin-offs filed under their own heading, plus any further entries that did not fit the series list.

RAWG's own platform list for this record is gamecube, nintendo-64. This catalog keeps only the Nintendo systems named above. A personal computer, or another company's console, on that list is not a claim that this page covers that version.
