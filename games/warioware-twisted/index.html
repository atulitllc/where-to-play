  • 2004
  • Game Boy Advance
  • Nintendo / Intelligent Systems
  • 32-bit handheld

History

Intelligent Systems hid a gyro sensor in the cartridge and turned the whole machine into the controller for WarioWare: Twisted! Nintendo published the Japanese Game Boy Advance release in 2004. Microgames last a few seconds, and many of them clear only when the plastic is tilted or twisted to the angle on screen.

Europe also received an official cartridge. North America did not, at the time, because that sensor made the hardware difficult to ship there. A prompt that says to rotate the device until a figure stands upright will not listen to the D-pad.

Wario still hurls a rapid stack of these tiny demands, sorted into hosted batches. Wrists replace the usual button mash, and blowing one microgame nicks a life in the chain. Intelligent Systems writes the jokes into instructions that are often as short as the games they introduce.

The territories that took the sensor got a real, official release from Nintendo. Players in North America simply had no local edition then, and the gyro was the obstacle. Japan and Europe's cartridges both ask you to tip the entire handheld, not to tap a button and pretend.
